The problem

Expiry dates live in three places, and nobody owns them.

Fictional example Bagtas Express runs 40 riders and 12 delivery vans. Like many small fleets, it tracks the papers that keep them legal by hand.

Three separate lists

HR keeps a spreadsheet, the fleet manager keeps a notebook, and each rider keeps the card in their wallet. None of them are connected.

Typed in twice

When a rider renews, HR updates the spreadsheet and the manager updates the notebook. If either one forgets, the lists stop matching.

Dates that disagree

The spreadsheet says Van 7 is registered until July. The papers in the glovebox say June.

What it costs

A van caught with lapsed registration means a ₱10,000 fine for the company, and the van can be impounded. A rider whose license lapses is off deliveries until it’s renewed.

Why these dates slip

Real · LTO rules

These are real LTO rules. They’re why a delivery company can’t track expiry dates from memory.

  • 5 or 10 years

    How long a driver’s license lasts, counted from the holder’s birthdate. Ten years is for drivers with no violations in the past five. Either way, it’s a long time to remember one date.

    Source: LTO, rules implementing RA 10930

  • Jan to Oct

    A vehicle’s registration month comes from the last digit of its plate: 1 is January, 9 is September, 0 is October. A fleet’s renewals are spread across the year, not on one date.

    Source: LTO, registration schedule by plate number

  • ₱10,000

    The fine for driving an unregistered vehicle, charged to the vehicle’s owner, plus ₱1,000 for the driver. It is the penalty behind the “No Registration, No Travel” rule.

    Sources: JAO 2014-01 on lto.gov.ph; Official Gazette, 25 March 2015

Checked 8 November 2026.

The solution

One record per document, and a reminder before it lapses.

Each license and registration is entered once, checked once, and watched until it’s renewed. Here’s what changes for the people at Bagtas Express.

  • Before: Three lists that disagree After: One record per document
  • Before: Someone has to remember to check After: Reminders at 30, 14, and 7 days
  • Before: Every renewal typed in twice After: The rider uploads the new copy once
  • Before: Lapses found at a checkpoint After: The manager is told the day it lapses

Bagtas Express’s solution

One tracker that reminds the rider, checks the new copy, and stops the reminders once it’s renewed.

What changes for each person

Approval flows simulated

Document owner

Riders and drivers

Gets reminders by email instead of finding out at a checkpoint. Uploads the renewed copy from a phone. If it’s returned, sees exactly why.

Manager

Fleet or operations manager

Checks each renewed copy against the record, then approves it or returns it with a reason. Hears about a lapse the day it happens.

Administrator

Runs the tracker

Sets the reminder schedule and approves new documents before they’re tracked. Reads the delivery report to catch emails that didn’t arrive.

We expect a bad photo

Most people upload a phone picture, so the file check and its error screen are part of the flow.

Failure has an audience

If the expiry date passes while the copy is still pending, the license expires and the manager is notified.

No dead ends

A returned copy comes with the reason, and Ana goes straight back to upload a corrected one.
